# Background
This file documents the intellectual foundations behind the HEROES framework. It exists to help Claude reason from principles, not just apply labels — understanding why each lens exists produces better critique than treating the framework as a checklist.
HEROES sits at the end of a clear line of inheritance: Rams → Maeda → Guyot → HEROES. Each step distilled the thinking of the one before it and added something new. Understanding this lineage makes the framework more than a list of lenses — it makes it a position.

## Dieter Rams — 10 Principles of Good Design (1970s)
Source: Developed over Rams' tenure as chief designer at Braun from the 1960s through the 1990s. The ten principles are widely documented and form the philosophical foundation of modern product design thinking.

### Core idea
Good design is not decoration — it is the removal of everything that is not essential. Rams believed that most products are damaged by the noise accumulated through over-engineering, fashion-chasing, and lack of discipline. His most cited principle captures the whole philosophy: good design is as little design as possible.
The ten principles are: good design is innovative, makes a product useful, is aesthetic, makes a product understandable, is unobtrusive, is honest, is long-lasting, is thorough down to the last detail, is environmentally friendly, and involves as little design as possible.

## John Maeda — The Laws of Simplicity (2006)
Source: The Laws of Simplicity, MIT Press, 2006. John Maeda is a designer, technologist, and former president of RISD. The ten laws are available in full at lawsofsimplicity.com.

### Core idea
Simplicity and complexity need each other. Removing everything produces emptiness, not clarity. The goal is to find what Maeda calls the point of maximum benefit: the minimum that is still satisfying. His ten laws move from tactical operations (reduce, organize, time) to perceptual principles (learn, differences, context) to a final synthesis.
